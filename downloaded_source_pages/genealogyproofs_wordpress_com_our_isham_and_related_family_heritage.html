Our Isham and Related Family Heritage

By: John Mark Browder, B.A., M.S., M.T.S., M.Div. (updated and corrected, Jan. 8, 2025)

The surname “Isham” comes from the Parish of Isham, Northamptonshire, England, near Kettering. It is pronounced “Eye-shum” and is derived from a brook called “Ise” which flows through the western part of England. The surname itself was, therefore, first found in Northamptonshire at Isham, a Saxon village and civil parish which dates back to 974 when it was listed as “Ysham”. By the Doomsday Book of 1086, the place name had evolved to the present spelling of Isham.

The place name literally means “homestead by the River Ise,” “ice home”, or “settlement by water”, having derived from the Celtic river-name + the Old English “ham” or “hamm”. The earliest record of the family was found here “where an elder branch of the existing family, Isham of Lamport, were seated soon after the [Norman] Conquest” of 1066.

It is understood, though perhaps not quite proven, that the Isham family line begins with a guy named simply Azo. Azo was born about 1060 and died about 1120 in Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England. He had a son named Roger who adopted as his surname, “de Isham“. Roger de Isham was born about 1100 and died about 1160 at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England. Roger de Isham had a son, Henry de Isham, who was born about 1140, presumably at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England, he also dying there in about 1200. Henry de Isham had a son, Thomas de Isham (c1180-c1250), born and died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England. Thomas de Isham had a son, Robert de Isham (c1220-c1280), born and died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England. Robert de Isham had a son, Henry de Isham (c1260-c1330), born and died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England.

Henry de Isham is the first in this line of whom we know a wife’s first name. She was Agnes (d. p. 1349). She also died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England. Henry de Isham and Agnes had a son, Robert de Isham (c1300-c1360), born and died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England. Robert de Isham had a son, Robert de Isham (c1335-1402), born and died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England.

Robert de Isham married Juliane (d. p. 1376), who also died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England. Robert de Isham and Juliane had a son, Robert de Isham (1377-March 13, 1423), born and died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England.

Robert de Isham had a son, Robert Isham I (1402-1475), born and died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England. Notice that he dropped the “de” from his ancestral name. He married Elizabeth Knostone (b. Irchester, Northamptonshire, England), daughter of Aston Knostone (b. 1373).  Robert and Elizabeth also died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England. 

The known children of Robert Isham and Elizabeth Knostone were William (1427-June 13, 1516), Robert II, Richard, and John. 

William Isham, son of Robert Isham and Elizabeth Knostone, married Elizabeth Brampspeth (1427-Sept. 20, 1478), daughter of Thomas Brampspeth (b. 1401).  Elizabeth was the widow of Robert Braunspeth.

William Isham was born and died at 1427 at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England.  Elizabeth Brampspeth was also born at Glooston, Harborough District, Leicestershire, England.  She died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England. 

The known children of William Isham and Elizabeth Brampspeth were Thomas (1456-1547), William, and John, all born at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England.

Sir Lord Thomas Isham, son of William Isham and Elizabeth Brampspsth, married Lady Ellen de Vere (c1456-c1547), daughter of Sir Richard de Vere (b. c1430, Addington, Northamptonshire, England) and Dame Isabella de Greene (b. 1434).  Isabella was a daughter of Walter de Greene and granddaughter of John de Greene and Margaret Greene.

Sir Lord Thomas Isham was born in 1456 at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England and died in 1547 at same.  The known children of Thomas Isham and Ellen de Vere were Henry, Eusaby and John. 

A digression into the ancestral heritage of the de Vere and related families follows.  I do not claim this to be completely accurate, but it is believed to be by other researchers. 

Alberic II held the title of Baron de Vere.  He married Lady Adeliza de Clare, daughter of Gilbert de Tousburg (second Earl of Clare) and Lady Adeliza (daughter of Hugh de Clermont, Count of Beauvois, and Lady Margaret, daughter of Heldwin IV, Count de Rouci, and Lady Adela).  Heldwin was the son of Elbo I, Count de Rouci, and Lady Beatrix, daughter of Rynerius XI, Count of Haniault, and Princess Hedwige.  Princess Hedwige was the daughter of Hugh Capet, King of France, and Adela, daughter of William, Duke of Aquitaine, and Adelheid.

Adelheid was the daughter of Otto I, The Great (son of Henry I, King of Saxony “The Fowler”), and Adelheid.  Otto was born in 912 and died in 973.  Adelheid, his daughter, was born in 951. 

Otto was crowned Emperor of the Holy Roman Empire in 936.  In 951 he crossed the Alps on a military campaign and declared himself to also be King of Italy.  He then married Adelheid, widow of Lothary (who had previously been King of Italy).  However, Otto was forced to return to Germany following his brief military campaign because, at that time, a series of revolts were taking place in Germany, as well as an invasion by the Magyars.  The Magyars were later crushed by Otto’s armies in the Battle of the Lech River in 955.  In 961 he crossed the Alps again in order to put down an uprising in Roma, Italy.

The son of Alberic II and Adeliza, Sir Robert de Vere, married Dame Matilda de Furnel, daughter of Robert de Furnel.  Their son, Sir Henry de Vere, married Lady (Dame) Hildeburga.  The son of Sir Henry de Vere and Lady Hildeburga, Sir Walter de Vere, married Dame Lucia Bassett.

The son of Sir Walter de Vere and Dame Lucia Bassett, Sir Henry de Drayton, married Dame Ivetta Bourdon, daughter of Sir William Bourdon.  Sir Henry de Drayton adopted the name “Manor.”  Sir Henry and Dame Ivetta had a son named Baldwin.  Sir Baldwin de Drayton married Dame Idonee Ginueges.  They in turn had a son, Sir John de Drayton who married Dame Phillipa d’Ardene.  And their daughter was Katherine Drayton.

Roger de La Zouche had a son, Lord Allan de La Zouche (d. 1269) who married Lady Eline de Quincy (1202-August 20, 1296), daughter of Roger de Quincy (1174, Winchester, Hampshire, England-April 25, 1264) and Helen de MacDonal of Galloway (m. 1202, Brackley, Aberdeenshire, Scotland), and grand-daughter of Lord Saier de Quincy II (b. 1150) and Lady Margaret de Bellmont (or Beaumont), daughter of Robert de Beaumont III (c1130, Leichester, Leichestershire, England-August 31, 1190, Duras, Greece), 3rd Earl of Liecester, and Petronilla Grentesmaisnill, (m. c1155).  Saier de Quincy II, son of Saier de Quincy I, was 1st Earl of Winchester.  Roger de Quincy was 2nd Earl of Winchester.  Helen de MacDonal was the daughter of Lord Alan of Galloway, son of Roland of Galloway and Helena of Morville, and unknown de Lacy, daughter of Roger de Lacy and Maud de Clare.  Roger de Quincy and Helen de MacDonal had another daughter named Margaret de Quincy who married William Ferrers and had a daughter named Joan who married Thomas Berkeley in 1267 and they in turn had a son named Sir Maurice Berkeley.

Lord Allan and Lady Eline de la Zouche had a son, Lord Eudo de La Zouche, who married Lady Millicent de Canteloupe.  They had a daughter, Lucy de La Zouche, who married Thomas de Greene II, son of Thomas de Greene I (b. 1292).  Thomas II and Lucy had a son, Sir Henry de Greene I (b. 1369), who married Dame Katherine de Drayton, daughter of John de Drayden.  Henry and Catherine had a son, Sir Henry de Greene II, who married Dame Matilda de Mandel, daughter of Sir Thomas Mandel.  Henry and Matilda had a son, John de Greene, who married Margaret Green, daughter of John de Greene.  John and Margaret de Greene had a son, Walter de Greene and a daughter, Isabella de Greene.

John de Drayden was the son of Baldwin de Drayden and Idonee Ginueges.  Baldwin de Drayden was the son of Sir Henry de Drayden.  Idonee Ginueges was the daughter of Ivetta Bourdon.  Sir Henry de Drayden was the son of Sir Walter de Vere (1304-November 5, 1387) and Dame Lucia Bassett (b. 1308), daughter of Gilbert Bassett (b. 1282).

Sir Walter de Vere was the son of Lord Henry de Vere (son of Robert de Vere) and Lady Hildaburga.  Lady Hildaburga’s mother was Matilda, daughter of Robert de Furnel.

Henry Isham, son of Sir Lord Thomas Isham and Lady Ellen de Vere, married Johan Busley, daughter of Robert Catalyne.  Henry Isham and Johan Busley had a son named Eusebius “Euseby” Isham (1486-1546).  This family also resided at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England where Henry was born and where they all died.

Lord Eusebius “Euseby” Isham, son of Henry Isham and Johan Busley, married Dame Anne Pulton (1495-Dec. 11, 1546), daughter of Sir Lord Giles Pulton (1460-1548) and Katherine Lovett (1464-1548).  Lord Euseby Isham is buried at All Saints Church, Pytchley, Northamptonshire, England.  Anne also died at Pytchley and is presumably buried at the same church. 

The known children of Lord Euseby Isham and Dame Anne Pulton were Giles (d. Aug. 31, 1559), Robert (d. 1564), Gregory (c1520-Sept. 4, 1558), John (c1525-Mar. 17, 1595/96), Henry (b. c1528), Katharina, Ellen, Elizabeth, Edith, and Isabel.

Lord Gregory Isham married Lady Elizabeth Dale on Dec. 16, 1548, at Saint Pancras Soper Lane, London, England.

Sir Eusebius “Euseby” Isham (Feb. 26, 1552-June 11, 1626), son of Lord Gregory Isham and Lady Elizabeth Dale, married Dame Anne Borlase (d. Dec. 1627), daughter of Sir John Borlase Sheriff of Buckinghamshire, Esq. (1526-May 6, 1593) and Dame Anne Lytton.  Sir Euseby Isham was born and died at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England.  He and Dame Anne resided at Braunston, Northamptonshire, England and later moved back to Pytchley.  Sir Eusaby is buried at Pytchley Church in Pytchley. 

The known children of Sir Euseby Isham and Dame Anne Borlase were Anne, John (d. Dec. 11, 1626), Mary, Euseby, William (Mar. 15, 1587-June 11, 1626), Sarah (d. June 10, 1627), Anthony (d. Jan. 26, 1603/04), Anne, Thomas (1600-Feb. 6, 1669/70), Susan, and Elizabeth (d. 1623). 

William Isham married Mary Brett (1603-1675), daughter of William Brett (d. 1624), at Toddington, Bedfordshire, England on August 15, 1625.  William was born at Braunston, Northamptonshire, England.  Mary was born at Hearne, Toddington, Bedfordshire, England.  Their known children were Euseby (1626-January 30, 1653/54), and Capt. Henry I (1628-December 1, 1676). 

Alexander Brett was born in 1504 at Whitstaton, Devonshire, England.  He married Rosemaderos, who was born in 1507 at Whitstaton, Devonshire, England.  They had at least one son, Robert Brett I, who was born in 1531 in England.  He married Elizabeth Bush, daughter of Edward Bush (b. c1508, England).  Elizabeth was born in 1534 in England.

Robert Brett I and Elizabeth Bush had a son, Robert Brett II, born about 1551 in London, County Middlesex, England.  He married Elizabeth Highgate, daughter of Reginald Highgate (b. c1529, London, England).  Elizabeth was born about 1555 in London, County Middlesex, England. 

Robert Brett II and Elizabeth Highgate had a son, William Brett (b. 1577 or 1583, Hearne, Toddington, Bedfordshire, England).  William’s wife was born about 1577, but her name is not known.  William died in 1624 in England. 

William Brett and wife had a daughter, Mary Brett, who married William Isham, as well as at least one son, Sir Edward Brett, who was knighted by King Charles I for his military excellence.  Sir Edward Brett left extensive property to the children of Henry Isham; Henry, Ann, and Mary.  

Capt. Henry Isham I, son of William Isham and Mary Brett, was born at Pytchley, Kettering Borough, Northamptonshire, England in 1628 and resided at Canterbury, County Kent, England.  He immigrated to Henrico County, Virginia about 1656, and built a plantation at Bermuda Hundred in Henrico County in 1661. 

It is said that Henry actually descended from a minor branch of the Isham family in England, which did not possess extensive land holdings there.  But once in the American Colonies, the family greatly prospered. 

Capt. Henry Isham I married Katherine Banks (c1627-1686), daughter of Christopher Banks (b. 1603, Canterbury, County Kent, England) on December 1, 1656 in Henrico County, Virginia.  Katherine was born at Canterbury, County Kent, England and later resided at Bermuda Hundred, Virginia.  She died at Amherst, Henrico County, Virginia.

Katherine had first married (1) Capt. Joseph Royall II (c1600-Mar. 10, 1655), marrying (2) Capt. Henry Isham I after Joseph’s death.  It happens that my line is descended from Katherine Banks through three family lines, the Isham family, the Jopling family, and the Eppes family. 

Capt. Henry Isham I became “one of tidewater Virginia’s largest landowners and the social arbiter of the families living on the south bank of the James River.”  Henry died on December 1, 1676 at Bermuda Hundred.   Katherine left a Will of her own dated August 10, 1686 in Henrico County, Virginia. 

The known children of Capt. Henry Isham and Katherine Banks were Henry II (d. 1678), Mary (1658/60-Dec. 29, 1735), and Anne Elizabeth (c1665, King and Queen County, Virginia-1718).  Note here that we are descended from both Mary and Anne.

Henry Isham II died at sea without having been married.  Anne Elizabeth Isham married Col. Francis “Frank” Eppes III (c1659-Jan. 1718).  Mary Isham was born at Bermuda Hundred, Henrico County, Virginia and died at Turkey Island in Henrico County.  She married Col. William Randolph I (Nov. 7, 1650-Apr. 11, 1711). 

The Will of Katherine Banks-Isham bequeathed to her grandson, William Randolph, £20, to her grandson, Henry Randolph £5, to her granddaughters Mary Randolph and Elizabeth Randolph, each “all such money that is now in the hands of my son-in-law [Col.] William Randolph”; the residue of money in the hands of her son-in-law, [Col.] William Randolph, to go to her daughters Mary Randolph (wife of Col. William Randolph) and Ann Eppes (wife of Col Francis Epes), equally; the above two daughters to be divided that which was on her trunk, with each to have two silver salt cellars. 

Her daughter, Mary Randolph, was bequeathed Katherine’s wedding ring, best feather bed, best silver tankard, and 15 shillings to buy a mourning ring. 

Her grandson, Joseph “Royal” (Capt. Joseph Royall III), received one servant named John Townes, for the time he had left to serve, and Katherine’s small silver tankard. 

Also, to “every child of my son Royal” (Capt. Joseph Royall III) were bequeathed two silver spoons.  Also, to her “loving son Joseph Royal, my best tankard”; to her grandson Richard Denis, one of her best cows and 2 silver spoons; to her grandson Isham Eppes (son of Col. Francis Eppes and Ann Elizabeth Isham), her “negro man Dick”; to her grandson Francis Eppes (son of same), her “best silver tankard; but one” to the child of her daughter, Ann Eppes, her “largest silver porringer and great cup”; her sealed ring, great hoop ring, pair of silver clasps, and silver bodkin were to go to her daughter, Anne Eppes. 

To her grandson, Richard Perrin (son of Richard Perrin and Katherine Royall), 1 feather bed and furniture; to her granddaughter, Sarah Royal, 1 yearling heifer; to her granddaughters, Katherine Farrar, Mary, Sarah, and Anne Perrin, each 2 silver spoons; to Katherine Farrar 1 Guinea; to Anne Perrin, one silver porringer; to her granddaughter, Sarah Dennis, 2 silver spoons and 1 pair of dowlass sheets;

To her two daughters, Sarah Wilkinson and Katherine Perrin, all her wearing clothes; to her “loving friend Mary Parker, 6 ells of best dowlas and as much of the finest serge to make a gown and petticoat”; to her grandson, Maiden Marshall, 1 two-year old heifer; to her son, Joseph Royall, all her land;

All of her crop of corn was to go to her executors, except enough to buy 2 gravestones, “one to cover me and other my dear decd. husband”.  All the rest was to be divided between her four children: Sarah Wilkinson, Joseph Royal, Katherine Perrin, and Anne Eppes, and to each 15 shillings for mourning rings. 

Executors were her son, Joseph Royall, and son-in-law Francis Eppes.  She instructed that her body was to be buried “near my dead husband on my own plantation”. 

Witnesses were John Worsham, Nathaniel Hill, and Littlebury Eppes.  The Will was probated on December 1, 1686 (1677-1692 Will and Deed Book, p. 392-301). 

A further digression of the Isham and de Vere families follows.  Obviously, all of this cannot be proven, but it is provided here just the same.

Lady Margaret de Bellmont was the great-granddaughter of Robert Beaumont II (1104, Leicester, Leicestershire, England-April 5, 1168, England) and Amice de Gael (m. after 1120).  Robert Beaumont II was the son of Robert Beaumont I and Isabel Vermandois.  Isabel was the daughter of Hugh Magnus and Adelhart de Vermandois.  Adelhart was the daughter of Herbert de Vermandois IV and Adele de Vexin.  Herbert IV was the son of Otho Vermandois (d. 1045) and Pavie.  Otho was the son of Herbert Vermandois III and Ermengarde.  Herbert III was the son of Albert Vermandois I (c917, Vermandois, Normandy, France-September 8, 987, St. Quentin, France-buried at Pas-de-Calais, France/m. before 954) and Gerberga (m. France).  Albert I was the son of Herbert Vermandois II (c884, Vermandois, Normandy, France-February 23, 942, St. Quentin, France) and Hildebrante.

Herbert II was the son of Count Herbert Vermandois I (c848, Vermandois, Normandy, France-948) and Countess Berthe Vermandois.  Count Herbert was the son of Pepin Quentin Vermandois II.  Pepin was the son of Berhard (d. 812) and Cunegonde.  Berhard was the son of Pepin, King of Lombardy (April 773-July 8, 810) (brother of Emperor Louis I the Pious of the Franks/ m. Judith of Bavaria) and Bertha de Toulouse.  Pepin I was the son of Charlemagne, King of France (b. April 2, 742, Aix-la-Chappelle, France/m. c771) and Hildegarde de Suabia of Alemmania, daughter of Rudapert.  Charlemagne was the son of King Pepin III “The Short” of the Franks (714, Lombardy, France-767, Milan, Italy) and Bertha de Laon II.  Pepin, King of Lombardy, France was the son of Charles Martel, King of France (689-741) and Rotrude.

Emperor Louis I the Pious of the Franks and Judith of Bavaria, daughter of Count Welf of Alemmania and Heilwig, had a son, Charles the Bald.  Charles married Ermentrude D’Orleans, daughter of Earl Vodon of Orleans and Engeltrude, and they had a daughter, Judith of the Franks, who married (1) King Ethelwulf of the Wessex and, later, (2) Count Baldwin I of Flanders in 862.  Judith and King Ethelwulf had a son named King Alfred the Great of England.  Judith and Baldwin had a son named Count Baldwin II of Flanders.                 Charles Martel was the son of Pepin de Mayor and Alpais.  Pepin was the son of Duke Ansgise (d. 685/m. before 639) and Begga.  Ansgise was the son of Saint Arnulf (b. about August 13, 582) and Dode “Clothilde”.  Arnulf was the son of Arnoul, Bishop of Metz and Oda de Savoy.  Arnoul was the son of Ausbert (b. 570) and Blithildes.  Ausbert was the son of Ferreolus, grand-son of Sigermerus I.  Sigermerus I was the son of King Clodius the Long-Haired (b. Westphalia, d. 455) and Basina de Thuringina.  Clodius was the son of King Pharamond of the Franks (c370-430) and Argotta.  Pharamond was the son of King Marcomir of the East Franks (c356-404).  Marcomir was the son of King Clodius I of the Franks.  Clodius was the son of King Dagobert II (303-379).  Dagobert was the son of Duke Genebald I of the East Franks.  Genebald was the son of King Dagobert I (d. 317).  Dagobert was the son of King Walter (d. 306).  Walter was the son of King Clodius III (225-298).  Clodius III was the son of King Bartherus.  Bartherus was the son of King Helderic (c185-253).  Helderic was the son of King Sunno (d. 213).  Sunno was the son of King Farrobert (d. 186).  Farrobert was the son of King Clodomir IV of the Franks (d. c186) and Hasilda.  Clodomir was the son of King Marcomir IV of Franconi (before 105-c149) and Athildis.  Marcomir was the son of King Odomir of the Franks (c85-c128).  Odomir was the son of King Richemer I of the Franks (c65-c90).  Richemer was the son of Ratherius II who was the son of Ratherius I who was the son of Antenor IV, son of Clodomir III (c3-c50), son of Marcomir III (c17 BCE-c 50 CE), son of Clodius II (c 37 BCE-c 20 CE), son of Francus.
