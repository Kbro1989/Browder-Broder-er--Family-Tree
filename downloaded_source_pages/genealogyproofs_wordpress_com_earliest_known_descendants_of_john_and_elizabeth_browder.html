Earliest Known Descendants of John and Elizabeth Browder

By: John Mark Browder, B.A., M.S., M.T.S., M.Div. (updated and corrected Dec. 30-31, 2024)

The known children of John Browder (c1686-p. 1741) and Elizabeth Jones (c1685- p. 1741) were Arthur (1715-c1738), Edmund (c1716-1759), John II (c1717-c1770), Richard (c1719-c1807), William (July 7, 1723-c1763), George (Feb. 5, 1731-1819), Anne (b. Feb. 13, 1733), and Joseph (February 2, 1741-March 1801).  All of these children were born in Prince George County, Virginia within Bristol Parish. It is possible they also had additional children. 

Arthur Browder (1715-c1738), the eldest son of John and Elizabeth Browder, probably married Mary Isham Randolph, daughter of Col. Thomas Randolph and Judith Fleming.  He and Mary will be dealt with more in another posting. 

Edmund Browder (c1716-1759), son of John and Elizabeth Browder, married Ann.  Some researchers state that Ann’s maiden name was Broadnax but I have been unable to corroborate this with extant records. 

It becomes obvious when consulting the available records that this Edmund Browder, son of John and Elizabeth Browder, had at least two children, Joseph (who lived past 1781) and Mason, as well as another son, who was also named Edmund.  Nothing is really known of Joseph except that he stood for the marriage of Lois Browder, daughter of his brother. Nothing at all is known of Mason Browder. It also becomes obvious through the process of elimination that Edmund Browder had yet another son named John, who would have been born in 1731/32. 

Edmund Browder, Jr. (c1734, Brunswick County, Virginia-1785, Chatham County, North Carolina), son of Edmund Browder, Sr. and Ann, served in the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War in North Carolina as part of the State militia (DAR Index, p. 90).  He is the one who married Ann Deloney (b. c1735), about 1752. 

It seems obvious that if Edmund Browder had been born about 1716, he would not likely have been able to serve during the war, although that is not a certainty.  And, does he and his wife really not have their first son before 1752 with them having their last daughter about 1774?  I think not.  This couple (Edmund Browder, Jr. and Ann Deloney) first resided in Brunswick County, Virginia where all of their children were born.  

Edmund Browder, Jr. purchased 362 acres of land on Poplar Creek from Robert Walker in 1773.  This property was bounded at one point by the property of Frederick Jones.  This deed was witnessed by John Neal, John Epes, Robert Hicks, Stephen Ellis, and Daniel Baugh, and was proven on June 26, 1780.  Edmund later sold this plantation, on December 30, 1779, to William Baugh, and moved, with his family, to Chatham County, North Carolina, where he purchased other land in April of 1780. 

Frederick Jones owned property that adjoined the plantation that Edmund sold.  Said property was deeded to Frederick Jones of Prince George County, Virginia by James Hall of Amelia County, Virginia on June 13, 1759, and was described as land “known by the name of the Indian old Field.”  It was also described as adjoining the properties of John Lewis, John Camp, Instance Hall, James Murray (of Prince George County), and William Hall.  James Murray himself had just deeded two acres of land (on June 12, 1759) to Charles Turnbull of Dinwiddie County, Virginia.  This property was that which Murray had previously purchased from William and Edward Mumford. 

According to Edmund Browder, Jr’s Will, the known children of Edmund Browder, Jr. and Ann Deloney were Darius (c1752-Nov. 1, 1813), Deborah (b. c1755), Silvia (c1759-c1835), John (c1760-July 4, 1818), Molly (c1762-c1709), Eunice (c1764-1829), Lois (c1770-c1833), and Mason (c1774-c1850). 

Edmund Browder, Jr.’s Will was written in June of 1781, and probated in 1785, while his two youngest daughters were still unmarried.  The Will stipulated that they be taken into the care of his brother, Joseph (so we know that Edmund, Sr. had a son named Joseph), and that they inherit their father’s property in Virginia.  Ann is also named directly in Edmund’s Will, as is Edmund’s brother, Joseph, whom he stated still resided in Virginia (Penland Browder, pp. x & 11).  So, we know that Joseph Browder remained in Dinwiddie County, Virginia. 

Edmund’s Will also stipulated that his youngest son, John, receive the three-hundred acre plantation which Edmund then resided on, and that another son, Darius, receive another three-hundred acre tract called “New Hope”.  Edmund’s wife, Ann, was to be maintained by the estate as long as she lived.  The will of Edmund Browder, Jr. follows: 

Anodomini 1781 June the (4?)

In the Name of God Amen, I Edmund Browder of Chatham County, State of North Carolina, planter, Being Weak in Body, but Whole in the Mercy of Almighty God of a Sound Mind and Being Convinced of the mortality of My Body, having Made My peace With God Through Jesus Christ, I Sollomly Commit my Soul into his hands as into the hands of a faithful Creator, And my Mortal Body after My Decease to the Care of My Surviving family and friends to be Laid in the Grave With Christian (Dignity?), in full Assurance of Hope And Joy In Expectation of Knowing the Same Gloriously Improved in the Morning of the Resurrection, And As for Those Temporal Goods And Blessings Which God W…. to Make me …. of …. First unto My youngest son John Browder I Bequeath the plantation I now Live on Containing three Hundred Acres of Land, one Negro Man Named Will, one Negro Woman Named Hannah, and one Boy…. , My Tender Loving Wife, to be Maintained out of it With …. She … With the Use of one Feather Bead (sic) [Bed] And … And Calves, (And Lastly?) I Give to My son Darius Browder the Tract of Land … New Hope Containing Three Hundred Acres, one Negro Boy…. one Bay …. to my two Daughters Lois & Mason all that is my property in Virginia to Be Equally Divided When Married or come of Age D…. my Brother Joseph Broder to take them and …. All the Residue of My Estate. I Desire to Be Equally Divided Amongst My Daughters Molly Clark, Eunice Willson, Silva …., Deborah Browder, …. to Be My Last Will & Testament…. Wills from Whence any other Might Arise. Given Under My Hand And Seal this (4?) Day of June one Thousand Seven Hundred and Eighty One.

      Edmd. Browder  Seal

John …. x …. Thomas Bell x  …. Wm. Wilson ….      ? Ramsey C. C. 

The original is difficult to read in several places, but the Will appears to name at least three other daughters besides Lois and Mason (who were known to earlier researchers), Molly, Eunice, Silvia, and Deborah.  My thanks to Dan Culp, a descendant of Molly Clark, for providing this information to me. 

In any case, Joseph Browder, brother of Edmund Browder, Jr., stood as security for the marriage of Lois Browder on December 21, 1792.  Lois Browder married John Chiles in Brunswick County, Virginia (Penland Browder, p. 11).  Mason Browder never married.  Mason Browder died in Roane County, Tennessee. 

The 1840 census of Roane County, Tennessee (Subdivision 20) lists Nancy Browder (70-79), along with one female [obviously Mason] (60-69), and three females (30-39).  One male slave (55-99) is also listed. 

The 1850 census of Roane County, Tennessee (Subdivision 20) again lists Mason Browder (76) as residing with head of household, Nancy Browder (80) and Mary Sherod (50).  Both Nancy and Mason Browder are listed as having been born in Virginia.  Nancy is said to have been Mason’s sister-in-law.  She was obviously Nancy Jane Crump-Browder, widow of Darius Browder. 

Darius Browder (c1752-Nov. 1, 1813), son of Edmund Browder, Jr. and Ann Deloney, married Nancy Jane Crump (c1770-Nov. 9, 1851), daughter of Joseph Crump (c1750-Aug. 1796), about 1783.  Their children were Edmund (c1784-Sept. 6, 1861), Martha “Patsey” (Feb. 3, 1787-c1857), Joseph (Nov. 14, 1789-Apr. 3, 1862), William (Feb. 10, 1792-June 29, 1890), Robert (c1793-June 1826), Frances “Frankie” (Apr. 1793-c1845), Elizabeth “Betsy” Jane (c1795-c1854), Mary (June 4, 1800-Dec. 25, 1868), John (Nov. 4, 1801-Feb. 29, 1888), and David (May 3, 1805-Apr. 26, 1876). The first seven children were born in Chatham County, North Carolina. Mary, John, and David were born in Lenoir Settlement, Roane County, Tennessee. 

The 1850 census of McMinn County, Tennessee (Subdivision 23) also lists “Edmond” Browder (66) [son of Darius Browder and Nancy Jane Crump], along with his wife, Lucinda (64).  This census states that Edmond was also born in North Carolina.  

The 1850 census of Roane County, Tennessee (Subdivision 20) again lists John Browder (48) [son of Darius Browder and Nancy Jane Crump], along with his wife, Minerva (36), and children; William (18), Darius (16), Samuel (14), Mary (11), John (9), Jane (7), Julia (6), Eveline (4), and Ellen (0).  This census states that John, Sr. was born in Tennessee. 

The 1840 census of Roane County, Tennessee (Subdivision 20) also lists “Dorias” Browder (30-39) [son of John Browder and Frances “Fanny” Richardson], as well as his presumed wife (20-29).  Also listed are two male slaves and three female slaves. 

The 1850 census of Roane County, Tennessee (Subdivision 20) also lists William D. Browder [son of William Browder and Elizabeth Lackey] (28), along with his wife, Sarah A. (27), and a boarder, J. Jolly (21).  This census shows that William was born in Tennessee. 

And the 1840 census of Roane County, Tennessee (Subdivision 20) also lists John Browder (30-39) [son of Darius Browder and Nancy Jane Crump], along with his presumed wife (20-29), and presumed children; one male (10-14), two males (5-9), two males (under 5), and one female (under 5).  Also listed are one male slave and one female slave. 

Deborah Browder, daughter of Edmund Browder, Jr. and Ann Deloney, married unknown White. It is not known if Silvia Browder married or had children. 

John Browder, son of Edmund Browder, Jr. and Ann Deloney, married Frances “Fanny” Richardson. Their children were Virginia Jane “Jinsey” (c1788-c1830), Nancy B. (Nov. 28, 1791-Feb. 1, 1885), Mary E. “Polly” (Aug. 6, 1795-Nov. 5, 1886), Julia “Judy/Judah” (Aug. 25, 1798-Nov. 6, 1869), James (c1800-1848), Elizabeth Eliza “Liza” (Apr. 14, 1800-1875), Darius (c1802-1848), Frances “Fannie” (Feb. 1804-Jan. 13, 1879), and Jeptha (c1806-1864). 

Molly Browder, daughter of Edmund Browder, Jr. and Ann Deloney, married Benjamin Burgess Clark (b. c1763). They had one known child, Darius Clark. 

Eunice Browder, daughter of Edmund Browder, Jr. and Ann Deloney, married unknown Wilson. Lois Browder married John Chiles. And Mason Browder never married. 

John Browder (c1731/32-1769), probable son of Edmund Browder, Sr. and Ann, married Amelia. Her maiden name is not known. They moved on to Bladen County, North Carolina. They had only one son, Thomas Browder (c1749-c1795), born in Bladen County, North Carolina. 

Regardless of whether we may ever find proof that this is correct, we do know that there was a John Browder who resided in Bladen County, North Carolina who was married to Amelia. And they fit this scenario perfectly for several reasons. First, the Bladen County, NC tax list for 1763 names Henry “Bosswill” (Boswell) together with “Jno.” Browder (Jno. was short for John back in that day) in the same entry. Also named in the same tax list was one David “Braveboy”. Keep in mind that all named parties would have been at least 21 years of age.

By 1770, Thomas Browder is found in the land tax records for Bladen County, NC. He purchased some land that year from Dennis Lennon. Then, in 1771, Thomas Browder sold some land located in Bladen County, NC to his half-brother, Christopher Sanders, demonstrating that his mother had already remarried. And on Nov. 11, 1779, Thomas Browder received a land grant of 200 acres in Bladen County, NC. Please note that John Browder is not found in this 1770 tax list, indicating that he was deceased by this time.

Thus far, then, we know that John Browder and Amelia had a son named Thomas, who would have had to have been at least 21 years of age by 1770, making his birth date about 1749.

We know that Amelia remarried after the death of John Browder to Thomas Sanders. Thomas Sanders wrote his will on Nov. 16, 1776 in Bladen County, NC (Deed Book 1738-1779, p. 5) in which he stated that he was “very weak & sick” leaving to his “bloved wife, Emilea” some assets as well to “my children” viz. Elizabeth Boswell, Elisabeth Ellis, Amelie Morley, Thomas Browder & “Elay Sanders, my daughter in law”. Witnesses were Duncan Morris and “Senvill [Samuel?] Hardwick.

On Apr. 27, 1778, Christopher Sanders himself writes his will (Bladen County, NC Will Book 2, pp. 259-60). In it he does not name his step-mother. He did name his wife, Sarah, as well as his two sons, Thomas and Christopher. He also names his daughters, Sarah, Amelia, Elvira, and Elizabeth.

In August of 1785, Amelia Sanders deeded her property to her son, Thomas Browder (Bladen County, NC Deed Book 1, p. 391). In this deed she also names her daughter, Lucy Ellis. This deed was also proven in August of 1785.

The 1790 census of Bladen County, North Carolina lists Thomas Browder (although in other typed versions he is mistakenly listed as Thomas “Browden”).

Arthur Browder married Charlotte in Bladen County, North Carolina about 1792. By about 1794 they had had their first son, Hardy Browder, born in Bladen County, NC.

On June 22, 1795 Thomas Browder deeded 200 acres of land to Jonathan Meares (Deed Book 7, p. 266). This deed states that said land was surveyed for Thomas Browder on Nov. 19, 1778, and granted on Nov. 11, 1779. This deed was proven in Sept. of 1799. 

Now, we know that the above Thomas Browder had a son named Thomas because on Dec. 11, 1807 Thomas Browder of Williamsburg, South Carolina filed a petition concerning the estate of “Thomas Browder Senr Decd” (Estates Box 4, Envelope 7, Probate Office, Williamsburg, SC). In the petition he mentions that some of Thomas Sr’s heirs are minors (but they are not named) and that the estate is “considerably in debt”. Thus, he asked for permission to sell said property at public auction. 

The response from David McClary, Esq. states that Thomas Browder, Sr. died intestate, so he had been living in Williamsburg County, SC when he died. 

Now, it seems clear that Arthur Browder (c1770-1825) was also a son of Thomas Browder, Sr., son of John Browder and Amelia. Thus, Thomas Browder, Jr. and Arthur Browder were brothers. To help tie this up, mention should be made that the 1850 census of Williamsburg, South Carolina lists the family of Moses Brayboy. Within this household are listed his wife, Jane, as well as Charlotte Browder (79 years of age). This census shows all persons in the household as having been born in South Carolina (although that may be a mistake concerning Charlotte). The reasons this is significant are (1) the connection to the “Brayboy/Braveboy” family previously found in Bladen County, NC, and (2) it is known that Arthur and Charlotte Browder had a daughter named Jane and it is obvious that she was the wife of Moses Brayboy. Those who claim that it was Charlotte who married Moses Brayboy have clearly misread this census. Again, it is provable that Arthur and Charlotte Browder’s first son was Hardy, so it is more likely that Charlotte’s maiden name was Hardy. 

The known children of Arthur and Charlotte Browder were Hardy, Sr. (c1794-1860), John (b. c1802), Joshua (b. c1804), Martin (c1807-p. 1880), Jane (b. c1815), Pvt. Isaac J. (c1816-p. 1880), and Pvt. William T. “Bill” (Oct. 18. 1819-c1921). 

John Browder II (c1717-c1770), son of John and Elizabeth Browder married Mary.  It is believed by some that Mary could have been Mary Cox.  I agree that this is possible, but have found no actual evidence myself.  This couple resided in Prince George (later Dinwiddie) County, Virginia, and within the boundaries of Bristol Parish.  

John Browder II patented 303 acres of land on Tommeheton Swamp in Amelia County, Virginia in 1748.  This tract was sold to John Merrimoon in 1753.  John also purchased 200 acres of land near Smoky Ordinary in Brunswick County, Virginia from Charles King of St. Andrew’s Parish on March 18, 1750 (BCDB 5, pp. 57-59).  He later purchased another 200 acres of land in Brunswick County, on November 28, 1752, on Lanier’s Branch from William Burrow.  From BCDB 5, pp. 57-59:

Indenture made 18 March 1750, between Charles King of St. Andrews Parish, Brunswick County, and John Browder Junr., of Bristol Parish, Prince George County, £30, 200a, on both sides of the Main road near the smoak (sic) Ordinary which said Land was granted to the said Charles King by Patent dated 1 June last past in this present year 1750 which joynes [George] Brewer’s Land.  Signed Charles King Senr. Wit: Roger Tillman, George Tillman, Lewis Parham.  Court 26 March 1751, Indenture acknowledged by Charles King, and Anne the wife of the said Charles personally appeared and relinquished her Right of Dower.  

John and Mary Browder sold 4 acres of land in Brunswick County in 1755.  In the same year, they sold another 150 acres to Benjamin Wheeler; 320 acres near Smoky Ordinary to Robert Gee; 200 acres to William Browder (John’s brother); and 200 acres to Isham Lucy.  That very day they also purchased 300 additional acres of land from Robert Gee, which they sold the next year to Robert Simmons.  In 1756, John Browder II made a purchase at the estate sale of Alexander Walker.  John Browder I also gave John Browder II some land in Prince George County in 1759 (Penland-Browder, p. 13). 

On September 20, 1768, John Browder II purchased 670 more acres of land on the south side of Sturgeon Run.  John Browder, then, had acquired as much as 1,300 acres of land in Brunswick County altogether (Penland Browder, p. 63).  Also, the following:

At a Vestry held at the Brick Church of Bristol Parish the 5th of January, 1760 (pp. 170-71): Ordered that the persons following be appointed to Prossission the lands in this parish in their several Precincts as Followith. . . .  That John Browder and Morris Vaughan with the freeholders of their precincts Prossession from Munks Neck Road up the north side of munks neck and Hatchers Run to the Parish line.

At a Vestry held at Blanford for Bristol Parish October 14th Day 1769 (pp. 218-19): To John Browder for Ann Hauks . . . . . 400  

The previously known children of John Browder II and Mary were Mason (a daughter/b. June 30, 1742), and Frederick (February 22, 1744-1815) (Penland Browder, p. 12).  It is entirely possible, even probable, that John and Mary Browder could have been married as early as 1736, or even earlier, which allows for the possibility that they could have had other children prior to these two, including John Browder III (c1752-1814).  And they certainly could have had more afterward too. 

John Browder III (c1752-1814), probable son of John Browder II and Mary, resided in Dinwiddie County, Virginia all of his life, as far as is known. He is listed over and over again as John Browder Sr. in the Dinwiddie County, Virginia land tax records.  He married Diana, whose maiden name is not known. 

John Browder III is first listed from 1787-1795 as owning 80 acres of land there.  From 1796 to 1799 he was again taxed for the same 80 acres and also for an additional 110 acres of land.  In 1800 he was taxed on 190 acres of land.  From 1801-1812 he was taxed on that 190 acres plus 114½ acres of land, conveyed by Robert Atkinson.  In 1813 he was taxed on 304½ acres of land.  In 1814 he was taxed on 277 acres of land.  Finally, in 1815 his estate was taxed on that same 277 acres of land, which was located on Hatcher’s Run, demonstrating that he was deceased by 1814/15. 

It is clear, according to the same tax records, that John Browder III (“John Browder Sr.”) had a wife named Diana (d. 1820) because from 1816-1820, she was taxed on a life interest of 80 acres of land, located on Hatcher’s Run in Dinwiddie County, which was conveyed by the estate of John Browder (Hughes, p. 51). It is clearly the same estate in all of these records. 

This same John Browder (45 and over – the oldest age category) had been listed in the 1810 census of Dinwiddie County, Virginia, along with his wife (45 and over), and one male (under 10).  Eight slaves are listed for this family that year.

The 1820 census of Dinwiddie County, Virginia lists Dianna Browder (45 and over), along with one male (16-25), and one male (10-15).  Also listed is one male slave and two female slaves.  So, it is clear from the censuses that John Browder “Jr.” had died. 

It is also now certain, because of extant records, that John and Diana Browder had at least one son, John Browder IV (c1771-c1817).  This John Browder, from 1816-1817, was taxed on 110 acres of land in Dinwiddie County, Virginia, conveyed to him by the estate of John Browder.  However, this John also soon died, as his estate was taxed from 1818-1820 on that same 110 acres of land, located on Hatcher’s Run in Dinwiddie County (ibid). 

This John Browder is the John Browder who married Elizabeth Dyson on September 29, 1796 in Chesterfield County, Virginia (Penland Browder, pp. 57-58).  The 1840 census of Sussex County, Virginia lists Elizabeth Browder [also listed in the 1820 census/widow of John Browder] (60-69), along with one male (20-29), and another male (5-9).  Also listed are five male slaves and four female slaves. 

John Browder (c1771-1817) and Elizabeth “Betsy” Dyson (d. c1823) had at least one son, John (known as John J. Browder), who was born June 20, 1806 in Dinwiddie County, Virginia and died Dec. 6, 1886 at Petersburg, Dinwiddie County, Virginia. He married Delphine “Delphy/Della” B. Puckett (c1808-Oct. 19, 1875) who was born in Dinwiddie County, Virginia and died in Matoaca, Chesterfield County, Virginia. 

They had several known children; Goodwin (b. c1830), John (b. c1832), Robert Henry (c1835-Jul 15, 1899), Eliza Ann (May 10, 1840-May 22, 1925), Elizabeth Martha (b. c1842), and Nathaniel Clenroy, Sr. (1848-Oct. 11, 1921). All children were born in Dinwiddie County, Virginia. 

Frederick Browder (feb. 22, 1744-1815), son of John Browder II and Mary, seems to have first moved to Lunenburg County, Virginia, and from there to Mecklenburg and Halifax Counties, Virginia, and still later from there, by 1790, to Nashville, Tennessee.  In about 1793, this family moved on to Franklin County, Tennessee (Penland Browder, p. 14/55).  He seems to have had a large family since there resided ten white persons within his family by 1793.  As far as I know, he was the first Browder, along with his family, to move to Tennessee. 

Frederick Browder is known to have married Elizabeth Blagrave (b. c1745) in 1765.  Their known children were Frederick A. (b. c1765-1831), William H. (b. c1767), Elizabeth Blagrave (b. c1769), Jane W. (b. c1770), Patsey W. (b. c1770), Mary “Polly” (c1773-1812), Sarah Tennessee (c1784-1812), Lucinda N. and Nancy C.  Note the substantial gap between 1773 and 1784. 

Fredrick Browder’s Will was probated in Franklin County, Tennessee in 1815 (Penland Browder, p. 14). 

Frederick A. (Arthur?) Browder, son of Frederick and Elizabeth Browder, it seems, married and had children.  He appears to have moved to Mississippi and later to Louisiana where he died.  Although it is not absolutely certain that the following is Frederick A. Browder, the 1820 census of Thompson Creek, Feliciana County, Louisiana lists Fredk. Browder (26-44), along with his presumed wife (16-25), and presumed children; two males (under 10), one female (10-15), and one female (under 10).  Also listed are five male slaves and two female slaves.  That the age range is a bit off is not unusual for a census, and there is no other known Frederick Browder to have been this person.  

Nothing further is known about Mason Browder (b. June 30, 1742), daughter of John Browder II and Mary. 

Richard Browder, son of John and Elizabeth Browder, married Mary Eppes Randolph, daughter of William Randolph, Gent. and Mary Eppes, and step-daughter of Dr. James Thompson.  More on this family in another section. 

William Browder, Sr. (b. July 7, 1723), son of John and Elizabeth Browder, married Margaret (c1723-p. 1780), about 1742.  William and Margaret Browder moved to Brunswick County, Virginia as early as 1758.  He sold 100 acres of land to William House in that same year.  The following deeds involve William Browder and/or Isham Lucy.  From BCDB 6, p. 323:

Indenture made the 28th day of November, 1758, between William Browder and William House, for £26, conveying 100 acres, adjoining lands of Isham Lucy, Robert Gee and Benjamin Wheeler.  Witnesses were Robert Gee, James Procter (his mark), and Henry Gee.  Indenture and Memorandum of Livery of Seizin were acknowledged in Court on February 27, 1759, by William Browder.

From BCDB 6, p. 439:

Indenture made the 8th day of August, 1759, between Randle Daniel and William House, for £50, conveying 538 acres, adjoining lands of Peter Daniel, Crablouse Creek, Francis Lett, and Kimble.  Witnesses were William Browder, Joseph Cannon, and Hugh Edwards (his mark).  Indenture and Memorandum of Livery of Seizin were acknowledged in Court on March 24, 1760, by Randle Daniel.

From BCDB 6, p. 548:  

Indenture made the 28th day of July, 1760, between Peter Daniel of Halifax County, North Carolina, and Robert Gee, Junr., for £20, conveying 150 acres, which was granted to the said Peter Daniel on August 10, 1759, adjoining lands of Brewer and King.  Signed by Peter Daniel (his mark).  Witnesses were John Tillman, William Smith and William Browder.  Indenture and Memorandum of Livery of Seizin were acknowledged in Court on July 28, 1760, by Peter Daniel.

Also, on October 24, 1761, Isham Lucy deeded 200 acres of land to John Gordon.  This land was bounded by lands of John Burrows and William Browder (BCDB 7, p. 27).  

Indenture made the 24th day of October, 1761, between Isham Luicy (sic) and John Gordon, for £50, conveying 200 acres adjoining lands of John Burrows and William Browders.  Signed by Isham Luicy (sic) (his mark).  Witnesses were Robert Gee, Robert Gee, Jr., and Swapeald Brewer.  Indenture was acknowledged in Court on October 26, 1761, by Isham Luicy (sic).

And, on February 22, 1762, William Browder deeded some land in Surry County to Thomas Lanier.  Margaret, his widow, and John (c1717-c1770), his brother, were Executors of William’s Will, which was probated in Dinwiddie County, Virginia. 

The known children of William and Margaret Browder were two known daughters, Margaret “Milley” (b. November 16, 1742), and Susannah (b. June 18, 1745) (Penland Browder, p. 12), as well as one son, William (c1755-by 1820).  Both of the daughter’s names could well indicate descent from both Arthur Jones III and Margaret, as well as Lt. Arthur Jones and Susanna. 

It is obvious that they had another daughter, Mary (b. c1760), who married into the Lucy family.  This is not only shown in court records concerning the estate of Jones Browder, but also in the following document. 

This indenture . . . January 12, 1781 between Matthew Smart and Mary, his wife, and John Moodie of Dinwiddie County of the one part and Buckner Harwell of Brunswick County of the other part . . . land in St. Andrew Parish . . . being part of one hundred acres formerly of William Browder now the property of his three daughters. . . .  William Fogg of Brunswick County, Meherrin Parish, William and James Huff and Allan Love all of Brunswick County and same parish sold land to William Fogg – George Browder’s land bounded it on the west, dated September 24, 1787, proved July 24, 1786.  May 18, 1783 – Robert Brooks Rose of Brunswick County, Virginia and Thomas Huff of same – To Thomas Huff – land in Brunswick County – George Browder’s land bounded it.

Margaret “Milley” Browder, daughter of William and Margaret Browder, I believe (but cannot prove), married William Crook II, son of William Crook I; and grandson of Solomon Crook (c1669-c1703) and Martha Wiley.  Nothing is known of Susannah Browder. 

William Browder (c1755-by 1820), son of William and Margaret Browder, married Elizabeth. They are known to have had a son, Jeremiah Browder (c1773-Dec. 20, 1854). He married Susannah “Susan” Clements.  Jeremiah and Susannah Browder, in turn had a son, Jeremiah James Browder (1816-c1860), who in turn had a son, Robert Callaway Browder (1842-1913), who in turn had at least one son, Jeremiah Columbus Browder (1869-1923), who in turn had a son, William Jesse Browder (1890-1959), who in turn had a son, Elmer Curtis Browder (July 20, 1915, Switchback, McDowell County, West Virginia-December 19, 2005, Batesville, Ripley County, Indiana).  

Jeremiah Browder (b. c1774), son of William Browder, married unknown.  The 1810 census of Rockingham County, North Carolina lists Jeremiah Browder (45 and over), along with his wife (45 and over), and three females (10-15), presumably their children.  It also lists five slaves.

Jeremiah Browder, having moved on after 1810, can also be found (45 and over) in the 1820 census of Iredell County, North Carolina, along with his wife (45 and over), and children; one female (16-25), one female (10-15), and one female (under 10).  No slaves are listed.

The family having moved once again, the 1830 census of Wilkes County, North Carolina lists Jeremiah Browder (50-59), along with his wife (40-49), and children; one female (20-29), and one female (10-14).  No slaves are listed.

The 1840 census of Wilkes County, North Carolina (Gilreath’s District) lists Jeremiah Browder (60-69), along with two females (20-29), and one female (under 5).  No slaves are listed.

The other known children of William Browder, Jr. and Elizabeth were Thomas (c1774-p. 1820), Isham (c1776-p. 1820), William III (b, c1777), and James (b. c1780). All of their children were born in Dinwiddie County, Virginia. It is unknown who any of the other children besides Jeremiah married. But it appears that William III may have been the William Browder who married Nancy Mitchell.

These brothers and their father first resided in Mecklenburg and Lunenburg Counties, Virginia.  William Browder, Sr. is listed in the Lunenburg County Tax List for 1782-1787 as owning one slave.

Elizabeth Browder relinquished her dower rights in a certain tract of land located in Dinwiddie County, Virginia about 1790 (Penland Browder, p. 189/DCOB 1, p. 27).  It appears that the William Browder family moved from Dinwiddie County, Virginia just prior to this date.

William Browder and his family moved to Greensboro, Guilford County, North Carolina.  The 1810 census of Greensboro, Guilford County, North Carolina lists William Browder (45 and over), as well as one male (16-25), and two females (16-25).  No slaves are listed.  The closest neighboring families to them in this census were Fuller, Hunt and Thomas.  Since William’s wife’s maiden name is unknown, it is possible that it could be of one of these families, who may have moved along with them.

The 1830 census of Iredell County, North Carolina lists Catherine Browder (40-49), along with one other adult female [William’s widow?] (70-79), and presumed children; one female (20-29), one female (15-29), and one male (under 5).  No slaves are listed. 

Isham Browder (b. c1775), son of William Browder, married unknown.  Isham Browder (26-44) is found in the 1810 census of Surry County, North Carolina, along with his wife (26-44), and children; one male (under 10), and three females (under 10).

Isham Browder (45 and over) is also found in the 1820 census of Iredell County, North Carolina, along with his wife (45 and over), and children, one male (10-15), one female (16-25), one female (10-15), and one female (under 10).  No slaves are listed.

The 1830 census of Iredell County, North Carolina lists Sarah Browder (40-49) [presumed widow of Isham Browder], along with children; two females (15-19), two females (10-14), and one male (15-19).  No slaves are listed. 

Thomas Browder (26-44) is found in the 1820 census of Iredell County, North Carolina, along with his presumed wife (16-25), and one female child (under 10).  One male slave and one female slave are also listed. 

It is my strong opinion, based on years of trying to sort this out, that William Browder, Sr. and Margaret had another son, the Isham Browder (June 12, 1762-Feb. 19, 1830) who was a Private in the Continental Army during the Revolutionary War and who later moved to Kentucky. Isham seems to have been born shortly before his father’s death.

In addition, I am now convinced that William Browder, Sr. and Margaret had two other sons, Jesse Browder (Nov. 19, 1758-Feb. 7, 1783) and Charles Browder (b. c1760-Oct. 1, 1777).

Previously, Jesse Browder’s parentage was unknown. All that was really known was that he served in the Revolutionary War and that he later married his cousin, Mary A. Browder, daughter of Richard and Mary Browder. It has become obvious to me that the only place Jesse really fits is here.

As for Charles Browder, I have determined that we should take the sworn testimony of Peter Eppes (c1759-Jul. 21, 1851) into greater account. The previous belief that Charles was a son of Richard and Mary Browder comes solely from family tradition. But the testimony of Peter Eppes, who knew both Jesse and Charles and who served in the same unit during the Revolutionary War with Charles, really should not be discounted as a mistake. And Charles fits quite well as a son of William, Sr. and Margaret too.

The evidence for both is detailed at length in the “Earliest Known Descendants of Richard and Mary Browder” section.

George Browder, Sr. (Feb. 5, 1731-1819), son of John and Elizabeth Browder, is known to have been the father of the three brothers, George, Jr., Uriah, and Isham, Sr.  He married Martha F. Parham (c1732-1821), daughter of Nicholas Parham (d. c1818).  It is entirely possible that the Isham name here came from the family’s close association with Isham Lucy. 

George Browder, Sr. patented 299 acres of land in Dinwiddie County, Virginia on the south side of Hatcher’s Run on December 15, 1758.  John Roberts purchased a neighboring tract of land to George Browder in 1769.  He also acquired 105 acres of land, some of which was in Brunswick County, probably through inheritance, which he paid taxes on (Penland Browder, p. 40).  

The 1810 census of Meherrin District, Brunswick County, Virginia lists George Browder [Sr.] (45 and over), along with his wife (45 and over), and one other male (45 and over) and presumed children; one male (10-15), two females (26-44), and one female (10-15).  One slave is also listed. 

In 1819-1820, Martha F. Browder was taxed on 100 acres of land and 77 acres of land in Dinwiddie County, located on Hatcher’s Run, conveyed by the estate of N. Parham by Will (Hughes, p. 52).  

The known children of George Browder, Sr. and Martha F. Parham were Isham Browder, Sr. (Jan. 12, 1767-Nov. 26, 1852), Uriah “Urias” Browder, Sr. (Sept. 14, 1768-1849), George Browder, Jr. (Oct. 8, 1770-Dec. 8, 1818), and, probably, William J. Browder, Sr. 

Isham Browder, Sr. (Jan. 12, 1767-Nov. 26, 1852) married Susanna Lewis (c1773-Jul. 27, 1852). They had one son, Isham Browder, Jr. (c1792-1850). Isham, Jr. is not known to have ever married or had any children. 

Uriah “Urias” Browder, Sr. (Sept. 14, 1768-1849) married Mary “Polly” Quarles (b. c1773). The known children of this couple were John (b. c1801), Catherine (Nov. 1802-Nov. 1885), Isham (1805-c1855), Uriah, Jr. (1812-Mar. 27, 1864), Ann R. (b. c1817), Susanna “Susan” (b. c1820), Pvt. George Edmund Browder, CSA (c1823-May 16, 1880), and Mary Jane. 

John Browder, son of Uriah Browder, Sr. and Mary Quarles, married Angelica/Angela F. Dameron (d. Jul. 11, 1862), daughter of Alexander “Alex” Dameron, on Mar. 8, 1834 in Brunswick County, Virginia. They had one known daughter, Rebecca Ann Browder (b. C1837, Brunswick County, Virginia) who married Benjamin Stevens Jones (b. c1835), son of John B. Jones and Elizabeth. 

Catherine Browder, daughter of Uriah Browder, Sr. and Mary Quarles, married Hartwell Ogburn. 

Isham Browder, son of Uriah Browder, Sr. and Mary Quarles, married Martha W. Burnett (c1817-p. 1870). Their children were William Allen (b. c1838), Lucilla/Lucinda “Lucy” L. (c1840-Oct. 27, 1878), Phillip (Jan. 11, 1841-Aug. 2, 1910), Lois (b. c1842), George W. (b. c1843), Joel (c1843-May 30, 1862), Martha Ann “Maggie” (Oct 27, 1843-May 25, 1917), Thomas Wilson (Feb. 1, 1846-Feb. 6, 1914), Miles N. (b. c1847), Marcus (b. c1848), Mary Etta (Oct. 7, 1850-Dec. 6, 1919), and Mildred Virginia (Sept. 30, 1853-Oct. 21, 1930). All children were born in Brunswick County, Virginia except for Mildred, who was born in Chesterfield County, Virginia. 

Uriah Browder, Jr., son of Uriah Browder, Sr. and Mary Quarles, married Susan “Sally” Elmore (b. c1820). Their children were Mary (b. c1842), Sarah Elizabeth (c1843-June 10, 1898), John H. (b. c1844), William T. (c1845-May 21, 1896), Harriet S. (b. c1848), and Charles (May 1853-June 1853). All children were born in Brunswick County, Virginia. 

Ann R. Browder, daughter of Uriah Browder, Sr. and Mary Quarles, married unknown Nolley. Their children were Anna J. (b. c1844), George M. (b. c1846), Mary F. (b. c1847), Marcus E. (b. c1850), Rebecca A. (b. c1852), and Needham M. (b. c1857). All children were born in Brunswick County, Virginia. 

Nothing more is known of Susanna “Susan” Browder or Mary Jane Browder, daughters of Uriah Browder, Sr. and Mary Quarles. 

Pvt. George Edmund Browder, CSA, son of Uriah Browder, Sr. and Mary Quarles, married Judith “Judy” Reese Hawthorne (c1833-Feb. 15, 1890), daughter of Peter Wilkins Hawthorne (c1802-p. 1870) and Phoebe “Fannie” Reese (c1802-Feb. 1835). Their children were Benjamin D. (b. c1853), Peter (b. Mar 1854), Mary “Mollie” Elizabeth (Mar. 6, 1856-Aug. 6, 1887), Virginia “Jennie” (b. Jul. 1858), James W. (b. Oct. 5, 1860), Oscar B. (b. Dec. 1, 1862), George “Georgia” (June 10, 1865), Emily Harrison (Sept. 5, 1867-May 30, 1925), Fannie S. (Jan. 13, 1870-Aug. 13, 1897), and Nannie Ella (Sept. 19, 1874-June 10, 1922). 

Anne Browder, daughter of John and Elizabeth Browder, married Henry Pettypool (b. c1730).  Their known children were John William (c1762-c1839) and Anderson (b. c1866). 

John William Pettypool married Nancy A. Browder (c1764-p. 1844), daughter of Richard Browder and Mary Eppes Randolph. Their children were Perkinson (c1792-June 11, 1836), Henry (b. c1794), Elizabeth (b. c1795), Stephen (b. c1798), John, David, and Peterson. Each child is listed without the second initial “P.” in court documents because this was done as a shortened form of the surname Pettypool.  Over time, many descendants simply dropped the longer surname Pettypool for “Pool”. 

Lt. Joseph Browder, Sr. (Feb. 2, 1741-Mar. 1801), son of John and Elizabeth Browder, resided in Brunswick County, Virginia at least from 1767-1801 (Penland Browder, p. 13).  Joseph Browder married Susanna “Sukey” Kelly (d. 1818), daughter of Giles Kelly.  And Joseph Browder was mentioned in the Will of Giles Kelly. 

This couple resided near Waqua Creek in Brunswick County, Virginia.  On September 28, 1759, according to the St. Andrew’s Parish Vestry Book, Virginia, Robert Briggs, Giles Kelly and Joseph Crook were ordered to do a procession of all lands between Waqua Creek, Flat Rock Rd. and Burches Rd. to the county line. 

Joseph Browder also purchased 100 acres of land from Drury Stith on June 9, 1767.  Giles Kelly was a witness to this deed.  Joseph later purchased another 18 acres of land on the upper side of Roses Creek from Colin Campbell. 

On March 28, 1769 (proven April 26, 1769), Samuel Burch and his wife, Elizabeth, of Brunswick County, Virginia, and St. Andrew’s Parish, deeded 225 acres of land to James Crook, for 60 Pounds, located on the south side of Hickory Run, which adjoined the properties of Averis, Morgan, McKinney, Richard Taliaferro, Peter Jones [son of Maj. Peter Jones and Dorothy Chamberlayne] and Davis.  Witnesses included John Edwards, Joseph Browder and Joseph Crook. 

Joseph Browder qualified as a Lieutenant in the Continental Army on February 2, 1779 (William and Mary Quarterly, Vol. 24, p. 105), but whether he saw any action during the American Revolutionary War is unknown to me since I have located no further record of his service.  One can only assume that he did, of course. 

Joseph and Sukey Browder also owned two slaves from 1782 through 1787, according to the tax lists for those years. 

Joseph Browder’s Will was dated January 20, 1801, and was filed in Brunswick County, Virginia.  He died in March of 1801.  His Will left practically his entire estate to “Sukey” (detailed at length by Sides-Dye).  Sukey died in 1818. 

The 1810 census of St. Andrews Parish, Brunswick County, Virginia lists Joseph Browder [Jr.] (16-25), along with his presumed wife (16-25), as well as one other male (16-25), one female (45 and over), one female (26-44), and one female (10-15).  Also listed are twelve slaves. 

The 1820 census of St. Andrews Parish, Brunswick County, Virginia lists Joseph Browder [Jr.] (26-44), along with his presumed wife (16-25), and presumed children; one male (under 10), one female (16-25), and three females (under 10).  Also listed are two male slaves and one female slave. 

The 1830 census of St. Andrews Parish, Brunswick County, Virginia lists Joseph Browder [Jr.] (40-49), along with his presumed wife (30-39), and presumed children; one male (10-14), one male (5-9), one female (15-19), and one female (10-14).  No slaves are listed. 

The 1840 census of Brunswick County, Virginia lists Joseph Browder (60-69), along with his presumed wife (40-49), and one male (under 5), and one female (20-29).  No slaves are listed. 

The 1850 census of Brunswick County, Virginia (Northern Division) lists Joseph Browder (71), along with his wife, Fanny (58), as well as Sally Browder (27), and Richard Browder (12).  This census states that Joseph was born in Virginia.  

The children of Joseph Browder and Susan Kelly were Sarah “Sally” (b. Mar. 17, 1767), Rhoda (c1769-1837), Mary kelly (c1771-c1797), Susannah “Susan” (c1774-c1850), John S. (c1775-Nov. 1859), Anica Erman (b. c1776), Pvt. Caleb P. (c1777-c1826), Joseph, Jr. (1779-1857), and Elizabeth (c1780-c1850). All children were born in Brunswick County, Virginia. 

Sarah, daughter of Joseph and Susanna Browder, married James Barnes. They had one known son, Henry.

Rhoda Browder, daughter of Joseph and Susanna Browder, never married.

Mary Kelly Browder, daughter of Joseph and Susanna Browder, married Sgt. Robert Lucy, Jr. Their known children were Robert Sterling and Moriah. 

Susannah Browder, daughter of Joseph and Susanna Browder, married (1) Ingram B. Collier (d. c1803). They had one known child, Ingram B., Jr. (Oct. 9, 1793-Nov. 10, 1841). After Ingram died, she remarried to (2) Jacob Payne, Jr. They had one known child, Joseph S. (b. c1813). 

John S. Browder, son of Joseph and Susanna Browder, married (1) Susanna Miller (b. c1776). Their known children were Joseph E. (c1802-Dec. 1849), Susan (b. c1806), Martha (b. c1810), John J. (c1813-c1867), Nancy R. (b. c1815), and Sarah. Please note that we have a John S. and a John J. It was common in those days for these initials to stand for “Sr.” and “Jr.”.

The 1810 census of Dinwiddie County, Virginia lists John Browder [possibly the son of Joseph and Sukey Browder] (26-44), along with his presumed wife (26-44), and presumed children; one male (under 10), one female (10-15), and three females (under 10).  Three slaves are also listed.

John Browder (45 and over) is also listed in the 1820 census of Meadsville, Halifax County, Virginia, along with one male (16-25), one male (16-18), one male (10-15), two males (under 10), one female (26-44), two females (10-15), and one female (under 10).  The female (26-44) could well have been a second wife, but is unlikely the one listed in the previous census.  Also listed are three female slaves. 

The 1840 census of Halifax County, Virginia (South District) appears to list the same John Browder (60-69), again along with his wife (60-69), as well as one male (15-19), one female (20-29), and one female (15-19).  Also listed are one male slave and two female slaves. 

The 1850 census of Halifax County, Virginia (Southern District) lists the above John Browder (75), along with his wife, Susan (74), and one presumed daughter, Nancy (40).  This census states that John was born in Virginia.

Joseph E. Browder, son of John S. Browder and Susanna Miller, married Jane Ferrell (b. c1802). Their children were John W. (Sept. 12, 1824-Jan. 4, 1862), Mary A. (b. c1826), Uphrasia America Frances (b. c1829), Washington “Wash” Randolph (c1832-1898), Julia J. “Julie” (June 14, 1832-July 4, 1890), Benjamin F. (b. c1834), Pitts C. (b. c1837), Jane E. (b. c1838), and Ephraim J. (Aug. 6, 1841-Mar. 26, 1903). 

Susan Browder, daughter of John S. Browder and Susanna Miller, married Gabriel B. Ferrell (c1803-Jan. 1885). Their children were Martha (b. c1827), Mary (b. c1834), Sarah (b. c1836), Louisa “Lou” Y. (Jan. 25, 1837-Nov. 5, 1884), John (b. c1842), and Gabriel Byrd (b. c1846). 

Martha Browder, daughter of John S. Browder and Susanna Miller, married James Lovelace (b. c1803). Their children were Mary (b. c1829), Eugenia J. (Feb. 15, 1834-Nov. 9, 1889), Susan (b. c1837), Mildred (b. c1840), James I. (1843-1904), Jacob (b. c1846), and Rhoda J. (Sept. 4, 1849-Feb. 21, 1924). 

John J. Browder, son of John S. Browder and Susanna Miller, married Elizabeth Anderson (c1815-p. 1880). Their children were Martha Frances “Winnie” (c1837-c1906), James C. (b. c1839), William T. (c1840-c1891), Nancy (b. c1842), Susan (b. c1844), Elizabeth (b. c1848), and Sarah “Sallie” M. (May 1853-Jan. 30, 1923). 

Nancy R. Browder, daughter of John S. Browder and Susanna Miller, married (1) unknown Arendall, (2) Isaac B. Terry (d. c1840), and (3) Needham W. Nolley (1815-June 1854). Nancy and Needham Nolley had one known child, Needham Nolley (b. Dec. 1854). 

Sarah Browder, daughter of John S. Browder and Susanna Miller, married unknown Franklin. 

After the death of Susanna Miller, John S. Browder married Paulina/Pearlina J. Strange (b. c1820). 

Anica Erman Browder, daughter of Joseph Browder and Susanna Kelly, married William Kennedy.

Pvt. Caleb P. Browder, son of Joseph Browder and Susanna Kelly, married Mary W. Scarborough (b. c1804). They had one known son, William Joseph Browder (c1813-Aug. 11, 1888). 

The 1820 census of St. Andrews Parish, Brunswick County, Virginia lists Caleb Browder (26-44), along with his presumed wife (26-44), and presumed children; one male (under 10), and one female (1625).  Also listed are one male slave and three female slaves. 

Caleb Browder died about 1826 since his property was inventoried that year (Penland Browder, p. 191).

Joseph Browder, Jr. son of Joseph Browder and Susanna Kelly, married Frances “Fanny” Johnson (b. c1792) in December of 1811. Their known children were William J. (b. c1822), Sallie A. (b. c1823), Joseph III (b. c1825), and John Richard (c1838-Jan. 19, 1906). 

Elizabeth browder, daughter of Joseph Browder and Susanna Kelly, married David Hampton, Sr. (c1774-c1840). Their known children were David Alphonso (b. c1814), Joseph H. (May 2, 1817-Nov. 13, 1863), Rufus L., Mary E., Louisa, Harmetheda, Ann, Richard C., and Emily.
