## Q001: PARENTS OF CAPTAIN ISHAM BROWDER (c.1732–1784)

**Status:** OPEN | **Priority:** HIGH

### What We Know
- **Captain Isham Browder** served in Guilford County, NC militia, 1779–1782
- Delegate to Fifth Provincial Congress of NC, Nov 1776
- 640-acre land grant (Entry #155, File #479, Grant #470), Jan 4, 1779
- Survey mentions **"John Browder's Corner"** — this is likely **John Browder (c.1760–1818)**, son of Edmund Jr. (1734–1785)
- Land was on south side of Wolf Island Creek, beginning at box elder (John Browder's corner)

### The Kinship Proximity Problem
John Browder (c.1760–1818) was the son of Edmund Jr. and brother of Darius (1752–1813). If Isham's land survey references John's corner, they were neighbors — likely kin. But the exact relationship is unproven:

| Theory | Likelihood | Evidence |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| Isham was son of one of John Sr.'s other sons (not Edmund Sr.) | Possible | Would make him cousin to Edmund Jr., uncle to John (c.1760) |
| Isham was nephew through a brother of John Sr. | Possible | Would place him in the same generation as Edmund Jr. |
| Isham was from a separate Browder line that arrived independently | Unlikely | The "John Browder's Corner" reference ties him to this specific family cluster |
| Isham was son of George Browder Sr. (1731–1819) | Possible | George Sr. had sons Isham (1767–1852) and Uriah (1768–1849), but this Isham is a different man (different dates, different county) |

### Critical Distinction
**Isham Browder Sr. (Jan 12, 1767–Nov 26, 1852)** = son of George Browder Sr. (1731–1819), lived in Dinwiddie County, VA.
**Captain Isham Browder (c.1732–1784)** = separate man, Guilford County, NC, died 32 years before the other Isham was born.

These are **two different men**. Ancestry.com and other databases have conflated them.

### Next Traces
1. **Guilford County Land Grant Patent Book 48, p.63** — examine original survey document at NC State Archives for additional names
2. **Guilford County Court Minutes 1768–1784** — look for Isham Browder as road overseer (appointed July 21, 1768) and any family references
3. **Rutherford Expedition rosters 1776** — check for Browder names in the same company
4. **Battle of Guilford Courthouse pension applications** — DAR records may list next-of-kin
5. **Dinwiddie County wills 1730–1750** — if Isham was son of a John Sr. brother, the brother may have left a will

## Q002: IDENTITY OF ELIZABETH, WIFE OF EDMUND I (c.1651–1739)

**Status:** OPEN | **Priority:** MEDIUM

### The Two Theories

**Theory A: Elizabeth Parsons** (John Mark Browder's current belief)
- Daughter of **Joseph Parsons** of Prince George County
- No direct documentary proof of marriage
- The name "Parsons" appears in early Virginia records near Browder holdings
- Would explain why no Bolling marriage record exists

**Theory B: Elizabeth Bolling** (earlier speculation, now doubted)
- Daughter of **Col. Robert Bolling** (1646–1709), Sheriff of Surry County
- Sheriff Bolling refused to arrest Edmund in 1693 — fined 630 lbs tobacco for dereliction
- This refusal strongly suggests kinship (father-in-law protecting son-in-law)
- However: no marriage record found; Bolling's known children don't include an Elizabeth who married a Browder
- Robert Bolling's will (1709) does not mention a Browder son-in-law

### The Evidence Chain
```
1693: Charles Goodrich sues Edmund Browder
      → Sheriff Col. Robert Bolling ordered to arrest Edmund
      → Bolling refuses/neglects
      → Court fines Bolling 630 lbs tobacco
      → Bolling was wealthy planter, not someone who'd default on duty casually
      → Kinship is the most plausible explanation
```

But WHICH kinship?
- Father-in-law (Elizabeth = Bolling daughter)?
- Brother-in-law (Edmund's sister married a Bolling)?
- Business partner (Bolling and Edmund had joint interests)?
- Political ally (Bolling was Quaker-sympathetic, Edmund may have been too)?

### The Parsons Connection
Joseph Parsons was a landholder in the same area. The name "Parsons" appears in Bristol Parish records. If Elizabeth was a Parsons, the Bolling arrest refusal needs another explanation — possibly Edmund's sister married a Bolling, or Edmund and Bolling were business partners in the tobacco trade.

### Next Traces
1. **Prince George County marriage bonds 1680–1710** — search for Edmund Browder or Parsons matches
2. **Robert Bolling's complete will and inventory 1709** — check for any Browder references
3. **Joseph Parsons land records** — proximity to Edmund I's 1704 quit rent acreage
4. **Surry County court records 1685–1700** — any Parsons-Browder joint transactions
5. **Bristol Parish vestry book** — Elizabeth Browder mentioned as witness or sponsor?

## Q003: WILMUTH BROWDER → BANISTER → CONTINENTAL CONGRESS THEORY

**Status:** SPECULATIVE | **Priority:** LOW

### The Theory
John Mark Browder proposes that Edmund I & Elizabeth had a daughter **Wilmuth** who married **Capt. John Banister** (son of Rev. John Banister & Martha Batte). Their son **Lt. Col. John Banister** (b.Dec 26, 1734) was:
- Continental Congressman from Virginia
- Signer of the Articles of Confederation
- Died 1788

If true, a Browder descendant was at the founding of the United States.

### Evidence For
- The name **Wilmuth** appears in early Browder family records (unusual name, likely passed down)
- The Banister family was prominent in Prince George/Dinwiddie County, same area as Browders
- Lt. Col. John Banister's birth date (1734) fits the generation of Edmund I's grandchildren
- The Browders and Banisters moved in the same social circles

### Evidence Against
- **No documentary proof** of Wilmuth Browder's existence
- **No marriage record** of Wilmuth Browder to John Banister
- Banister family genealogies do not list a Browder mother for Lt. Col. John Banister
- This may be a case of "same area, same era, wishful thinking"

### Verdict
Compelling narrative. Zero proof. File under "investigate if primary sources emerge."

### Next Traces
1. **Banister family papers** (Virginia Historical Society) — any Browder references
2. **Prince George County deed books 1720–1740** — Wilmuth as grantor/grantee
3. **Dinwiddie County wills** — any Banister will mentioning Browder kin
4. **DAR applications for Lt. Col. John Banister** — may list maternal lineage

## Q004: BIRTHPLACE OF EDMUND BROWDER I

**Status:** OPEN | **Priority:** MEDIUM

### The Three Theories

**Theory A: Born in Virginia (Favored)**
- Son of George Browder (c.1624, Dublin records 1642 + Virginia records 1655–1665)
- George was in Virginia by 1655; Edmund born c.1651/1652
- First record: 1682 Surry County tithables, age ~30
- If born in Virginia, he was a **second-generation colonist**, not an immigrant
- This debunks the "three brothers" immigrant tradition

**Theory B: Born in Ireland**
- George Browder in Dublin 1642
- Edmund could have been born in Ireland, immigrated as young man
- Would make him first-generation immigrant
- No Irish birth record found

**Theory C: Born in Wales**
- Older family tradition (now largely abandoned)
- No documentary support
- Likely confusion with other Browder families

### Why It Matters
If Edmund I was born in Virginia, the entire Browder-American narrative shifts from "immigrant pioneers" to "colonial gentry" — the family was already embedded in the English colonial system before the 18th century. This changes the psychological architecture of the lineage.

### Next Traces
1. **Charles City County records 1650–1660** — George Browder land transactions, possible birth of Edmund
2. **Jamestown/James City County parish registers** — 1651–1655 baptisms
3. **Surry County tithable records 1660–1680** — George Browder listed? If so, with dependents?
4. **Headright records** — if Edmund was imported, someone claimed headright for him

## Q005: THE BROWDERER LINE (1327–1604)

**Status:** OPEN | **Priority:** HIGH

### The Reconstructed Chain
```
John Browderer (~1327, King's Sergeant at Arms, London)
    → [gap: ~90 years]
William Browderer (~1419, named in Baron Hungerford's Will 1459)
    → [gap: ~50 years]
Richard Browderer (~1466, Coldharbour Cornwall 1502)
    → [gap: ~55 years]
Gilliam Browderer (~1521, Tudor Subsidy Roll 1541, "Stranger")
    → [gap: ~80 years]
Robert Browder (c.1604, Manchester Cathedral 1623)
```

### The Gaps
- **1327 to 1419:** 92 years. Could be 3–4 generations. No records found.
- **1419 to 1466:** 47 years. Could be 2 generations. No records found.
- **1466 to 1521:** 55 years. Could be 2 generations. No records found.
- **1521 to 1604:** 83 years. Could be 3 generations. No records found.

### What Would Prove It
- **Guild records** from the Worshipful Company of Broderers (pre-1558)
- **Manchester Cathedral marriage/baptism records** 1580–1625
- **Lancashire parish registers** for Browder/Browderer variants
- **Apprenticeship records** — Browderer apprentices in London 1500–1600

### Next Traces
1. **Guildhall Library, London** — Broderers' Company records pre-1558
2. **Manchester Cathedral archives** — marriage register 1623 (already confirmed), plus surrounding years
3. **Lancashire Record Office, Preston** — parish registers for Manchester, Salford, Bolton
4. **The National Archives, Kew** — apprenticeship records (TNA series IR 1)
5. **College of Arms** — any Browderer arms petitions before 1558

## Q006: DESCENDANTS OF EDMUND W. BROWDER (c.1699–1771)

**Status:** OPEN | **Priority:** LOW

### What We Know
- Edmund W. had 3 children: **Handstress** (1722), **James** (1725), **William** (1727)
- All baptized at Bristol Parish
- Edmund W. died impoverished in 1771; parish paid for grave digging
- "Browder's Island" (Fort Henry site) was sold or lost

### Why It Matters
Edmund W. lived on the site of **Fort Henry**, the southernmost English outpost in Virginia, commanded by **Maj. Gen. Abraham Wood** and later **Maj. Peter Jones** (whose daughter Jane Rolfe married **Robert Bolling**). The Browders were literally on the frontier's edge — the same frontier impulse that later drove Darius to Tennessee and Albert to Oklahoma.

If Edmund W. has living descendants, they carry the Fort Henry geographic legacy.

### Next Traces
1. **Chesterfield County deeds 1750–1800** — sale of Browder's Island, heirs mentioned
2. **Bristol Parish register post-1727** — marriages of Handstress, James, William
3. **Prince George County wills 1720–1770** — any mention of Edmund W.'s children
4. **Dinwiddie County tax lists 1770–1800** — Browder descendants

## Q007: GENETIC VALIDATION OF JOSEPH → ALBERT LINE

**Status:** OPEN | **Priority:** MEDIUM

### The Problem
The 1860 census shows **Albert Browder (b.1825)** as a child in the household of **Edmund Browder (c.1784–1861)** — his uncle. This created decades of confusion: was Albert Edmund's son or nephew?

John Mark Browder resolved this through McMinn County wills: Edmund's estate lists "Albert, born 17 Jul 1825" as a nephew (son of brother Joseph), not a son. Joseph's will confirms Albert as heir.

But **genetic proof would close the loop permanently**.

### What Would Prove It
- Y-DNA match between a documented descendant of **Joseph (1789–1862)** and a documented descendant of **Edmund (c.1784–1861)**
- If they match as **nephew/uncle** (not brothers), Joseph → Albert is confirmed
- Autosomal DNA triangulation between descendants of Albert's siblings and Joseph's other children

### Next Traces
1. **FamilyTreeDNA Browder surname project** — check for existing Y-DNA kits
2. **AncestryDNA/23andMe** — identify descendants of Joseph's other children (John F., Simeon E., Julia, Mary Denton)
3. **McMinn County Historical Society** — any living descendants of the Denton line (Mary Browder + Isaac Denton)
4. **FindAGrave** — contact memorial managers for Joseph's children
